Another nice surprise for the project: the open-source prototype of the **Smallest**, shared on Printables, was featured on **[Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44289276)**, under the title *"3D printable 6" f/5 compact travel telescope model."* The thread climbed to the front page quickly and racked up dozens of comments.

## What the discussion covers

As is often the case on Hacker News, the comments go straight to the practical questions:

- **Where to source the optics?** Several commenters recommend picking up a secondhand Skywatcher Dobsonian to get a 150/750 mirror set, quality the community describes as "consistently good."
- **Which print material?** PETG comes up repeatedly as the recommended choice over PLA, which is seen as too heat-sensitive for outdoor use. The debate continues over the merits (and potential brittleness) of carbon-fiber-filled filaments.
- **How durable are printed parts, really?** A whole sub-thread digs into plastic durability: the consensus is that a properly printed PETG part, with at least 25% infill, holds up well for functional components.
- **Cost, documentation, actual viewing quality.** Other questions cover the real build budget, how clear the documentation is, and whether the project delivers on observation quality, beyond mechanical elegance.
- **Advice for beginners.** Several experienced amateur astronomers suggest newcomers try an astronomy club first before jumping into a DIY build — a nice reminder that the Smallest speaks to tinkerers and curious newcomers alike.

## Open-source version vs. commercial version

As with the Hackaday coverage, one useful clarification for anyone discovering the project through this thread: the version freely available on Printables is the Smallest's **original prototype**, not the final version. It's the design that proved the concept and built a community around it, but many features added since then — mechanical refinements, finish options, compatible accessories, and more — aren't part of it.

The version sold in the shop reflects how the telescope has evolved since, with those improvements built in. Both paths make sense: print the open-source prototype to understand and experiment with the design, or go with the commercial version to get all the improvements right away.
