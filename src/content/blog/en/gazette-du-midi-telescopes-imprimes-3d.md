On September 18, 2026, **La Gazette du Midi** published an article by Maxime Cossé about **La 3ème Dimension**: *"With his 3D-printed telescopes, Raphaël Labro wants to make astronomy accessible to everyone"*.

## Astronomy for everyone

The article starts from a simple observation: long reserved for a community of insiders, astronomy is now opening up to a wider audience. From the Tarn-et-Garonne, the workshop contributes with 3D-printed instruments that are affordable and designed to be understood.

That's the whole idea behind **Lumi**, the refractor delivered as a kit to assemble yourself:

> "This approach lets users understand how the product works, and the different movements performed by the lens."

## Handcrafted, analog, and nomadic

The article also covers the workshop's low-tech approach:

> "The telescopes I make are not only handcrafted, they're also analog, which means they can be carried anywhere."

And what 3D printing makes possible:

> "This process makes it possible to produce very complex parts and reach the quality of telescopes made by major manufacturers."

The journalist also mentions **Smallest**, whose open-source plans on Printables have passed 23,000 views, and **Picco**, the next nomadic telescope expected in 2027.
